Privacy Policy

ViralLab · last updated 24 October 2026

In short: ViralLab is a private tool used by one person to analyse their own social media content. It has no other users, collects nothing from anyone else, and sends no data to any third party.

Who this covers

ViralLab is operated by Gabriele Baldocci as a personal research tool. It is not a product, not a service offered to the public, and has no user accounts. The only person whose data it processes is its operator.

What data is accessed

With the operator's explicit authorisation, ViralLab reads, from the operator's own accounts only:

  • basic profile information (user.info.basic)
  • account statistics such as follower and video counts (user.info.stats)
  • the operator's own published videos and their public performance metrics — views, likes, comments, shares (video.list)

It does not request, and cannot access, private messages, contacts, drafts, or any other person's account.

Why

To study which characteristics of the operator's own videos are associated with better performance, and to test whether recommendations derived from that analysis measurably improve outcomes. This is personal research into the operator's own work.

Where it is stored

All data is stored on hardware the operator owns and physically controls: a private server and a workstation on a private network. Nothing is hosted with a cloud provider, and the system is not reachable from the public internet.

Sharing

None. Data is not sold, shared, published, transferred to any third party, or used for advertising. There are no analytics trackers, no advertising networks and no data brokers involved.

Retention and deletion

Data is kept while the research continues and deleted when it is no longer needed. The operator can delete all stored data at any time, and revoking the application's access from the platform's own settings stops all further collection immediately.

Your rights

Because the only data subject is the operator, rights of access, correction, deletion and portability are exercised directly against the local database. Platform access can be revoked at any time from the account settings of the platform concerned.
